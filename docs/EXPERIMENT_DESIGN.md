# Hypothetical Experiment Design (no results)

> **This document is a plan, not an analysis.** The RavenStack dataset has no experiment ID, variant, treatment/control assignment, exposure or experiment dates. No A/B test was run on this data, and nothing here reports experimental results. Only the baseline rates and account inflow below come from the data. Effect sizes are design assumptions.

## Why an experiment is needed

The observational analysis found:
* the largest trial-funnel drop-off is after conversion: 23.4% of converted trial accounts are flagged as churned;
* directional churn gaps by channel and industry, none significant after multiple-testing correction.

Observational data cannot show whether an intervention *reduces* churn. A randomised test can.

## Proposed test: guided 90-day onboarding programme

| Element | Design |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | Accounts that receive a structured 90-day onboarding programme (CSM kickoff, feature walkthroughs, day-30/60 check-ins) are less likely to churn early than accounts receiving the current experience |
| Unit of randomisation | Account (avoids contamination between seats of the same customer) |
| Eligibility | Newly signed-up accounts at first paid subscription; exclude internal/test accounts |
| Assignment | 50/50, stratified by plan_tier and referral_source (channel and plan are plausible prognostic factors) |
| Primary metric | Churn event within 90 days of signup (binary) |
| Secondary metrics | Account churn_flag at 180 days; distinct features used in first 90 days (requires fixed event timestamps); MRR at day 90 |
| Guardrails | Support tickets per account, CSAT, refund amount, CSM cost per account |
| Analysis | Two-proportion z-test (intention-to-treat) plus logistic regression with the stratification variables; 95% CI for the absolute difference; sample-ratio-mismatch check; no peeking before the planned sample (or a pre-specified sequential design) |
| Duration | Fixed by the sample size below plus the 90-day outcome window |

## Prerequisites (from the data-quality review)

1. A single source of truth for churn: today `churn_flag` and `churn_events` disagree for 62.4% of accounts.
2. Usage events timestamped consistently with signup and subscription dates, so onboarding-period behaviour can be measured.
3. Logged assignment and exposure (variant, timestamp) for every eligible account.

## Implication

At current pilot volume, only very large effects (about 10 pp) could be detected, and that would take more than two years. Recommended options:
* run the test after public launch, when signup volume is higher;
* use a higher-frequency leading metric once event timestamps are fixed;
* treat a small pilot as qualitative learning rather than a significance test.
